**R4: one design, with my part being the final rendering state before any compile.** I accept L's acquisition graph and J's staged assembly, with these GPU facts built in. (1) With G1 the environment is a texture that arrives with the lightmaps, so every live material is in its final state as soon as the GLB, maps and env are in; nothing is compiled twice. (2) On Android, where the parallel-compile extension is effectively absent (0.21% of contexts, **M2**), `linkProgram` still returns immediately and the GPU process links in order; the main thread blocks at the first synchronous query, and because commands execute in order that first query waits for *every* link queued before it. three's `compileAsync` resolves after a 10 ms timer there (`WebGLRenderer.js:1551–1567`; `WebGLProgram.js:997–1005` marks programs ready when the extension is missing) and the block lands in `onFirstUse` (`WebGLProgram.js:863–879`: `getProgramInfoLog`, `LINK_STATUS`, then uniform and attribute reflection). So the order that overlaps compile with CPU on Android is: submit all final programs in one batch, then do the CPU-only work (foliage, hulls, audio decode, which issue no GL sync calls), then the first-use draws behind the loading screen. Texture and buffer uploads can be interleaved; they do not block. (3) Warm the lazy variants before START with one hidden draw each: the clump material, the mask and occluder override programs (with the foliage fix in R10 they become their own programs), the screen-on material. My forecast share is unchanged from round 2 in substance but re-based on J's passive numbers: G1 1.1–1.4 s net on the emulation, G10's loader half 0.3–0.4 s, G2 nothing separate; I accept **15.5 s / 13.5 s** as the panel's assumed figures with a 13–15 s envelope for all tracks, Android unmeasured.

1. **Before any timed run, record the context:** `WEBGL_debug_renderer_info` UNMASKED_RENDERER and VENDOR, `chrome://gpu` (ANGLE backend: GLES or Vulkan, and the driver version), `getSupportedExtensions()` filtered to `KHR_parallel_shader_compile`, `EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2`, `EXT_color_buffer_half_float`, `EXT_color_buffer_float`, `OES_texture_float_linear`; `MAX_TEXTURE_SIZE`; `devicePixelRatio`; the display rate from 60 rAF intervals; the canvas size the engine picks (`setPixelRatio(min(DPR, 2))`, `engine.ts:130`).

### R7: G3 design and the recording spec

**Cadence.** `setAnimationLoop` keeps running at the display rate; inside `render(time)`, when the room is idle, return early unless `time - lastRendered >= 1000/30 - 2`. That yields every second vsync at 60 Hz, every third at 90, every fourth at 120, without counting frames and without drift (no accumulator; a late frame simply moves the next one). `lastFrame` is only updated on frames that render, so `dt` accumulates correctly across skipped vsyncs. Reduced motion: render on demand only (clock tick, portrait frame, lantern, tree or cube state changes), with motes and sway frozen, which is the point of that setting.

**Active means display rate.** Any of: a pointer event in the last 300 ms; a captured pointer, `fingers`, `turning` or `slash` non-empty; `|spin| > 0.0005`; `watering > 0`; falling clumps or bits alive; any GSAP tween alive (`gsap.globalTimeline.getChildren(true, true, true).length > 0` covers hover, outline, focus, cube lift and joy, radio switch, lantern, boot, dive and return); `mode !== 'room'`; plus 500 ms of hysteresis after the last of these clears. Everything that is physically integrated per frame therefore runs at full rate, so the cadence never touches it.

**What has to become time-based anyway.** `parallax.lerp(pointer, 0.05)` (`engine.ts:1048`) is per frame: at 30 fps the settle takes twice as long. Replace with `parallax.lerp(pointer, 1 - 0.95 ** (elapsed * 60))`, where `elapsed` is the unclamped frame interval capped at 0.25 s; it reproduces today's feel at 60 and is identical at any rate. `turnTree(spin)` (`engine.ts:1033`) applies the coast per frame while `spin` decays per second, so a 30 fps idle coast would turn half as far; use `turnTree(spin * elapsed * 60)`. Reset `lastFrame` in `updateLoop()` on visibility resume so the first frame after a hidden tab does not see a huge interval.

**The `dt` clamp.** `dt = min(0.05, t - lastFrame)` (`engine.ts:1026`). At 30 fps the interval is 33 ms and the clamp never bites: no change is required for 30. At a nominal 20 fps the real interval is 50–54 ms, so water, clumps, bits and the spin decay run up to 7% slow; measured 18.7 fps (J) is inside that. If 20 is ever adopted: compute `elapsed` as above, use `dt = min(0.1, elapsed)` for the simulations (free fall at 0.1 s Euler steps is fine for clumps), and keep those simulations in the active set so they run at full rate regardless.

**How the owner judges it.**
1. *Live first.* A scratch build with a `?idle=60|30|20` query flag, flipped on the owner's phone and desktop. A live flip at full screen is the only test that is not limited by a recorder.
2. *Recordings at 60 fps or not at all.* Android's built-in recorder often captures at 30 fps, which cannot show the difference; use `adb shell screenrecord --bit-rate 20000000 /sdcard/idle-A.mp4` (captures at the display rate) or OBS/Game Bar at 60 on the desktop, never DevTools screencast. Same fixtures for every take: seed is built in, fixed `Date`, the same saved tree, lantern warm at 1.0, same window size.
3. *Script, each take 45 s:* 10 s still wide view (motes, sway, portrait 10 Hz, clock 2 Hz); hover on and off an object (outline fade); a pointer sweep to the edge, release, 3 s settle (parallax); a turntable flick and its coast; 3 s of watering; one cut and the clump's fall; dive and return. Takes at 60 and 30 (and 20 if asked), files labelled A/B blind.
4. *Judge at real speed first,* full screen, segments 1 and 3 are the ones that can differ; then at 0.25× (`ffmpeg -filter:v setpts=4*PTS`) as a diagnostic only. The active segments must be identical by construction. Accept 30 if the owner cannot pick A from B at real speed in the still and settle segments.
5. *Numbers attached to each take:* renders per second counted inside the loop, main-thread % over the still segment, and the first-render latency after the pointer moves (should be one vsync).

### R8: foliage compaction contract

**Layout (33 bytes per vertex, J's):** position Float32×3, anchor Float32×3, normal Int8×3 normalized, color Uint8×3 normalized (linear), shootId Uint16 non-normalized, node Uint8 non-normalized. Source facts that make it safe (**M**, three 0.186): integer-typed attributes are bound with `vertexAttribPointer` (float conversion) unless the type is INT/UNSIGNED_INT or `gpuType` is `IntType` (`WebGLBindingStates.js:357`), so `attribute float shootId;` and `int(shootId + 0.5)` in the hook keep working unchanged and are exact to 2^24; bounds and raycasts go through `fromBufferAttribute` → `getX`, which denormalizes (`BufferAttribute.js:423`), so normalized normals and colours are safe wherever three reads them back. Per-frame vertex fetch 20.2 → 11.9 MB (**S**); the vertex shader still runs all 360,426 vertices every frame whatever the growth state, because hidden nodes are collapsed by the reach clamp rather than skipped; that fixed cost is the next thing to attack after compaction (sort nodes so a `drawRange` can cover "reach ≤ n"), not now.

**Colour.** The greens are four discrete linear colours scaled by a continuous shade (0.26–1.0) and lerped to the new-growth colour over three nodes, so colours are continuous, not a palette. Linear 8-bit quantises the darkest interior (linear 0.011 in the deepest shade) to about three levels, which is roughly four output levels apart after sRGB encoding before lighting adds to it; on needle-sized triangles under three lights and the environment map that is unlikely to band, but it is exactly what the flip test must look at. Never store sRGB bytes and read them as linear. Fallbacks, in my order: sRGB-encoded bytes with `sRGBToLinear` in the `color_vertex` replacement of the foliage *and* clump hooks (same bytes, one output level of resolution in the darks), then Uint16 linear (36 bytes). J's order is the reverse; either passes.

**Normals.** SNORM8, 0.37° maximum error (J); invisible on roughness 0.8 by argument, gated by the ×8 diff like everything else.

**Worker: no, until measured.** The writer should emit the final typed arrays directly (no `number[]` staging of 1.08 M entries per attribute). After L's graph the generation overlaps the lightmap transfer, which is at least 2.5 s at 1.6 Mb/s for 500 KB, so it leaves the critical path without a worker; a worker would buy loading-screen smoothness and the warm-cache desktop case only. Gate as J proposes: keep one only if a single uninterrupted task over 50 ms remains on the handset.

**Code paths that must follow the layout.** `clump()` (`bonsai.ts:423–441`) slices arrays into plain `BufferAttribute`s and drops `normalized`: construct with `(slice, itemSize, source.normalized)`. The mask and occluder passes use `scene.overrideMaterial`, which ignores the foliage hook (see R10); positions stay Float32 so the override pass draws the right coordinates, but the reach clamp is missing there with or without compaction. The outline, warm-up and any future worker transfer must all use the final attribute types.

**Screenshot acceptance.** Fixed seed (11, built in), frozen `swayTime` and `Date`, three storage fixtures (fresh neat tree, J's trimmed legacy fixture, 30-day overgrown). Views: bonsai close-up at warm, red 1.5 and blue 1.5 (the foliage takes the lantern's point light), wide, turntable at 90° and 180°, a clump mid-fall at a fixed time after a cut, and the hover outline on the bonsai. Steps: exact integer IDs must be pixel-identical to today (`magick compare -metric AE` = 0; the shader sees the same floats); packed normals and colour must be within A's thresholds (max channel ≤ 25, ≤ 0.35% of pixels over 8 in close-ups) plus a 2× crop of the crown's shaded interior with the ×8 diff for the owner's eye on the dark greens; then the owner's flip.

## 2. Final ranked list for the GPU and runtime track

Savings are not additive across experts. "Forced-sync" link figures are desktop GPU-process numbers; "first-query" figures are what the main thread actually waited (passive hook). Nothing here needs a Blender re-bake.

1. **G1 — ship the environment as an 8-bit sRGB cubeUV WebP, size 256, q95, exported from the engine's own capture point; drop runtime `fromScene`.**
   - Saving: the `fromScene` stage is 2.04–2.35 s on the emulated phone with A's assets (**M**): 1.06–1.20 s of first-use stalls for the 16 programs linked inside it (the GGX convolution 0.68–0.80 s, the Gaussian blur 0.08–0.09 s), 0.57–0.61 s of the scene's first texture uploads, 0.06 s of buffer uploads, and 0.3–0.45 s of face renders and JS. G1 removes the PMREM programs, the duplicate no-envMap variants and the face renders, **1.2–1.5 s on this emulation (S)**, and moves the uploads to the first render rather than removing them; Android unknown but the GGX program is the room's largest shader. +44,506 B on the wire (**M2**), about 0.2 s at 1.6 Mb/s (**S**). GPU memory −3.1 MB kept, −6.3 MB transient (**S**). Removes the three PMREM programs and the four no-envMap duplicate variants.
   - Quality risk: low on appearance (66–74 dB lossless, 52–64 dB q95 on the rendered frame, **M2**); the owner approves `shots-r2/d2-*` (already in the review set). Medium on process: it must be exported from the engine's capture point (a careless re-capture is 32 dB off).

Verify: no `PMREM*` link in the per-program list and seven fewer programs; no `RGBA16F` 768×1024 allocation; PSNR ≥ 50 dB on the four views against the current build; the three owner pairs.

2. **G3 + G4 + G5 — 30 fps idle by elapsed time, lazy trail, START ring on the compositor.**
   - Saving (**M**, controlled A/B this round): the ring costs 10–23 points on the phone; the 60 → 30 cap removes 35–40% of the ring-off idle cost (21–37 points); the trail is 3.4–4.1% of idle samples. ## 4. Remaining disagreements for the adjudicator

1. **R3:** the 88% → 65% idle drop should be struck as an asset effect; book G3 against the controlled numbers above, where the cap's gain is a fraction (35–40%) of whatever the ring-off idle cost is on the day.
2. **GPU timer queries under CPU throttle are not GPU time.** This round's "GPU" p50 of 4–12 ms per frame at 4× is submission time; anyone citing such numbers for the phone should use frame intervals or an unthrottled context.
3. **The outline-mask foliage defect** (R10) should land before G9, because it changes what an "identical outline" means in the acceptance set; it is small, but it is not an optimisation.
4. **R8 colour fallback order** (sRGB bytes before 16-bit linear): minor; J's order is acceptable.
5. **Android credit for G1 is unknown:** the PMREM GGX program is the largest shader in the room and probably the longest link on a phone, but 13.5 s is an emulation budget until the USB session reports the first-query stalls.
